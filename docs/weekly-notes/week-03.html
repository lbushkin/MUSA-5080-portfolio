Week 3: GGPLOT & Addressing Margins of Error

Using ggplot for data visualization and addressing margins of error in data
Published

September 11, 2026

What We Did

Went over the basic elements and syntax of using ggplot for data visualization. Discussed how to properly address margins of error in ACS data.

Key Concepts

Visualizing data is important because different datasets can have similar (or even identical) summary statistics but look very different (i.e. Ansombe’s Quartet).

  • A plot is a mapping from columns in a dataset to visual properties on a page. Known as “Grammer of Graphics” (“GG”).
  • Ex: estimate column -> position on y-axis, population column -> postition on x-axis

GGPLOT Basics plot1 <- ggplot(data) + this creates a blank canvas with your data as reference aes() + despite the name, “aesthetics” refers to column assignment geom_something() shape/type of chart

“Decorations” (i.e. colors, etc.) go in geom_*() portion of code, not aes()

Options for geom:

  • histogram
  • point (for scatterplot)
  • boxplot
  • col (for barchart)
  • line
  • errorbar

Combining 2 geom:

  • Each type has its own aes specfications
  • Ex: geom_col() has aes( x = somevalue, y = somevalue), while geom_errorbar() has aes(ymin = somevalue, ymax = somevalue)

Extra:

  • facet_wrap() for creating multiple charts side-by-side for comparison
  • coord_flip() to flip x and y axis orientation
  • mutate(str_remove(column, “__“)) to remove some text/edit a column
  • Can use theme_minimal() for graphs as a shortcut for nice aesthetics
  • labs() for the labels of a graph

Census Bureau uses coefficient of variation (“CV”).

  • CV is a relative measure of sampling error, tied to the 90% confidence interval.
  • Census assumes a low CV of 15% (high reliability), medium of 15-30%, and high > 30% (low reliability, high sampling error)

Derived uncertainty

  • Cannot simply add up or multiply margins of error when comparing different estimates
  • In tidycensus: moe_sum() will properly add moe’s for different estimates
    • Also: moe_prop() for one estimate as a share of another, and moe_ratio() for ratio of 2 variables

Change over time with ACS data

  • 5-year ACS estimates have overlapping time periods when comparing years that are close together (ex: 2023 and 2022)
  • Instead, use ACS years that do not have overlapping periods (ex: 2018 and 2023)
  • Standard error of difference and confidence interval of change to determine if the difference in estimates between years is real (i.e. does not include zero)
